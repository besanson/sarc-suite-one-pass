arXiv submission metadata
==========================

Title (verbatim from the paper)
--------------------------------
One Gate Is Not Enough: Composing Stateful Pre-Action Controls for Agentic AI

Author line
-----------
Gaston Besanson (Universidad Torcuato Di Tella)

Category suggestion
--------------------
Primary: cs.AI (Artificial Intelligence)
  Rationale: the paper's four primary contributions are (1) formalizing
  remediation-induced control coupling -- a remediation applied by one
  pre-action control can change the action, evidence, or context another
  control evaluates, invalidating that control's earlier judgment; (2) a
  remediate-and-regate composition protocol that restores per-action
  soundness after one remediation, in the scoped one-shot/idempotent
  setting this artifact implements; (3) an empirical demonstration that two
  implemented remediation operators do not commute, so remediation order
  becomes governance semantics rather than an implementation detail; and
  (4) governance-state contamination -- a governed evidence buffer whose
  current admissibility does not imply future reference trustworthiness,
  and two mitigations that reduce but do not eliminate that exposure. The
  join-semilattice non-compensatory verdict semantics and the unified,
  lineage-preserving Evidence Set are supporting results, not the paper's
  central claim. This is squarely an AI-systems and formal-semantics
  contribution.

Cross-list: cs.SE (Software Engineering)
  Rationale: the artifact is a deterministic, open-data reference
  implementation with pre-registered hypotheses, exhaustive finite-model
  checkers, a 30-seed statistical sweep, and machine-checked/human-reviewed
  proof-status tagging for every claim -- an engineering-methodology
  contribution (empirical software engineering rigor) as much as a
  theoretical one.

Cross-list: cs.CY (Computers and Society)
  Rationale: the paper's subject is governance of agentic AI systems --
  authority, resource, and evidence-validity controls, and the honest
  reporting of what composition does and does not cover -- directly
  relevant to AI governance and accountability.
